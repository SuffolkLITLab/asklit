Massachusetts Tenant Rights: A Quick Reference Guide

SECURITY DEPOSITS
A landlord may collect a security deposit equal to one month's rent. The deposit must be held in a separate interest-bearing bank account. The landlord must provide a written receipt and the bank account information within 30 days. Within 30 days of a tenant moving out, the landlord must return the deposit with interest, or provide an itemized list of deductions.

REPAIRS AND HABITABILITY
Under Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 111, Section 127, landlords must maintain rental units in a safe and habitable condition. This includes providing heat (at least 68°F from September 15 to June 15), hot water, working locks, and freedom from rodents and insects. Tenants may withhold rent, repair-and-deduct, or report to code enforcement if a landlord fails to maintain habitability.

EVICTION PROCESS
A landlord must give written notice before evicting a tenant. For non-payment of rent, the notice period is 14 days. For other lease violations, it is 30 days. A landlord cannot lock out a tenant, remove belongings, or shut off utilities without going through the court process. The tenant has the right to appear in court and contest the eviction.

RENT INCREASES
There is no statewide rent control in Massachusetts. A landlord must give at least 30 days' written notice before increasing rent for a month-to-month tenant. For tenants with a lease, rent cannot be increased until the lease term ends unless the lease allows it.

RETALIATION
It is illegal for a landlord to retaliate against a tenant for reporting housing code violations, organizing other tenants, or asserting legal rights. Retaliatory actions include raising rent, reducing services, or threatening eviction within six months of the tenant taking protected action.
